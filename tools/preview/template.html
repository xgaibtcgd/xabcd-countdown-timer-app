Morning Mission · v0.7 art direction

Every graphic that isn't a buddy, drawn in code

The seven buddy PNGs are the app's good artwork. Everything around them was emoji text or flat vector fill. These are their replacements, drawn with the same soft clay treatment the characters have — one light source from the upper left, a same-hue core shadow, a bright rim on the lit edge, a soft contact shadow, and no outlines anywhere. Pick a buddy to re-tint the set.

Buddy

The five passes

One shape, built up. The third pass is the one that stops it looking like clipart.

Routine icons

Twelve tasks, on a chip tinted to the chosen buddy. The objects keep their natural colours — tinting them too gave a blue sun.

These replace emoji text. Emoji render differently on every manufacturer's build, and three of the characters the old build used — ‖ ☷ ⌂ — are not emoji at all, but rare Unicode that shows as an empty box on some devices.

At task-row size

The real test: 56 px on a 1080 px-wide phone, five to a screen. If an icon needs more than four parts, it stops reading here.

Collectibles

One a minute along the trail, up to twelve. Not-yet-reached items are desaturated rather than faded — at 27% opacity they vanished against the reef.

Goals

The destination at the end of the trail. Four of the seven have a hinged part that opens when the mission is finished.

Interface glyphs

Drawn flat, not modelled: chrome should read crisp beside the modelled objects, and most of these are small.

Palettes

Sampled from the buddy artwork. Before this, every accent in the app was one of three literals shared by all seven characters.

BuddyPrimaryAccentLight tintInkCollects

Shark and cloud are both hue 203. Sampled straight from the art their light tints were nearly the same colour, which would have made their cards indistinguishable side by side in the picker. They are now separated by saturation: shark's stays a visibly aqua wash, cloud's is nearly white.

Rendered from the same float[] geometry the app compiles into Paths, exported by tools/ExportArt.java. Not a mockup — if a shape is wrong here, it is wrong in the build.
